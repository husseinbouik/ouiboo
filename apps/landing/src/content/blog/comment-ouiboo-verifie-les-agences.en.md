Ouiboo is a technology platform: agencies sell their own trips, and travellers pay the agency directly. Before an agency can publish trips on Ouiboo, it passes a verification gate. This article walks through what is checked, step by step — and what verification does not claim.

## The travel licence

The agency submits its Law 11-16 travel licence (organiser-distributor — ODV — for agencies that organise trips). The licence number, issue date and issuing authority are recorded, and the licence document is checked against the issuing authority's list where one is available.

## Civil-liability insurance

The agency's RC (responsabilité civile) insurance certificate is recorded — insurer, policy number and validity dates — and must be in force for trips to publish.

## Seller identity

The agency's legal name, registration and address are recorded, so the seller of record shown to travellers is the real business behind the listing.

## Expiry is enforced

When a licence or insurance policy expires or fails verification, the agency's listings pause automatically — there is no grace period.

## What verification does not promise

Verification states what was checked and when; it is not a guarantee of any trip's outcome. Ouiboo provides the technology; the agency is the seller of the trip and is responsible for delivering it.
